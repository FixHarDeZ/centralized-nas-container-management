# Frame check — defect classes, and running the gate when vision reads fail

Standing depth for SKILL.md step 10 (per-shot review). Step 10 owns WHAT to sample and the delivery format; this file owns the defect classes that decide the verdict and the workflow when `vision_analyze` itself is failing.

## The identity sheet (code-gated since 2026-11-19)
- `clipjob.py idcheck <job> K --raw <file>` writes `shot<K>_id.jpg` (up to 2 refs + frames at 5/25/45/65/85 % and
  the last frame, one row) and prints `ask_vision`. Ask that question AND this file's questions (Flow text, sub
  band, scale, planned action, presenter identity) in ONE `vision_analyze` of the sheet. `ready` refuses a Flow
  shot with refs until the sheet of THAT file exists and `--identity match|drift:…|count:…|presenter:…` is given.
  The value is exactly `match`, `drift:<product part>`, `count:<a part count that differs — compartments,
  buttons, pieces>` or `presenter:<how her face/hair differs from the portrait>`: prose after `match:` is refused.
  `count:` cannot be fixed in post, so the review puts 🔁 first (onesuper-basket 2026-11-22 shipped 2
  compartments vs 3). `presenter:` words the review line as the face, not the product (eucerin 2026-11-19).
  **📝 shots skip all of this** — Fix's reply is his review (`prompt-pack.md`); this file is for 🤖 shots and
  the post frame sheet.
- **Pixel check (2026-11-25, 0 vision): `idcheck` also runs `scripts/prodcheck.py`** — it locates each ref in the
  shot (SIFT) and compares the product with ITSELF over time, band by band. `pixel.verdict: change` names the band
  and seconds (cal-d-protein-1mo: the boxes' navy bottom band went brown/pink at 3 s; the vision read said MATCH and
  misnamed the box). The question then asks about that band; `ready … --identity match` is refused until you add
  `--pixel-ok "<the hand / shadow / glare over it>"`, a real change is `--identity drift:<part, from s>`.
  `shot<K>_prod.jpg` = the product straightened at those moments. `no-match` (round, glossy, soft products) =
  nothing measured, not a pass. It cannot see a product wrong from its first frame — that stays the vision read.
- **This read is the GATE, not a discretionary check — it runs even when `note`/`status` prints `vision N/budget … no more vision_analyze`.** That warning exists to stop the extra rounds (crop locators, chip zooms, a second opinion on a question the pixels already settled); an `--identity` verdict cannot be invented and `ready` refuses without one, so spend the read on the sheet, then `note` the overage and name what you cut to stay near budget.
- A product that changes BETWEEN frames is drift even when each frame looks plausible (aukey-pby92: silver →
  thick dark box with a big display → white, built-in cable turned into a loose one). DRIFT goes into the review
  as `❌ identity` with the part named; Fix decides.
- **A DRIFT verdict read off the idcheck SHEET is a screening, not the verdict — and its usual false positive is
  shot framing.** The sheet pairs a large ref tile with ~150 px shot tiles, so a wider shot (product smaller in
  frame) reads as `base shape / post thickness / head size / product size changed` on every tile at once while the
  structure is identical. Before recording `--identity drift`, pair the ref with one FULL-resolution frame of that
  shot (same height, side by side) and ask the structural question only — parts present, counts, proportions of the
  product against ITSELF — explicitly splitting the two: `is the product RESHAPED, or just SMALLER in this shot's
  framing?` Smaller-in-frame = framing, record `match`; a part that differs on the full-res pairing = real drift.
  **The same screening misreads COLOUR at tile size.** A ~150 px shot tile turned a blue-grey plaid with a teal
  lining into `monochrome grey — matches the OTHER reference` on two shots of the same job, while the full-res
  ref-vs-frame pair read the identical colourway both times: so a sheet verdict of `wrong colourway` / `wrong
  lining colour` goes through that same full-res pairing (ask for the exact plaid and lining colours there)
  before it becomes `--identity drift`. On a worn garment the lining can be in shadow or simply out of view at
  that frame — that is `match`, not a colour flip.
  Conversely a full-res pairing that finds every part matching overrides the sheet's drift call the same way —
  the pixels decide, never the tile read. The confirmation runs BOTH ways: a part the tile claims is PRESENT is
  not cleared until the full-res pairing shows it either.
  **The same screening turns a WORN garment into a HELD one** — a ~150 px tile of a presenter in a hoodie read
  `she holds the hoodie up` while full-res frames showed it on her body (the chip outfit she no longer wears
  still read as her outfit at tile size). For apparel, put `is she WEARING it, or HOLDING it while wearing
  something else?` on the full-res pairing alongside the structural question, and let that answer overrule the
  sheet: wearing vs held decides both the identity verdict and whether the planned action (wear/fit beats) happened.
- **The product's own mechanism moving as the board planned is NOT drift** — a clicker's halves springing apart,
  a lid opening, a fold unfolding all change the silhouette between frames while the object is doing exactly what
  the storyboard asked. Check the planned-action answer in the same read before flagging: drift is a part or
  appearance changing that the board did NOT plan (colour flip, port gone, thickness changed), not the object
  performing its action.

## Defect classes
- **A contact sheet's own filename/timestamp labels are annotations, not on-screen text — say so in the question.** Otherwise the reader reports `f1.jpg @ 0.5s` as a caption the video drew, which reads as a text defect that does not exist. Name the labels as sheet furniture in the prompt ("the text above each frame is my label, not part of the video"), and treat any text answer that quotes a filename as the annotation, not the clip.
- **A geometry verdict read off a SHEET is not a verdict — confirm it on the pixels of the actual frame.** The sheet's own black label bar sits directly above each tile, so a reader reports `hair cut flat by the top edge` on a frame whose top rows hold zero dark pixels, and the same furniture reads as a dark border or a shadow. Before any "cut / touching the edge / clipped" claim enters a review, crop that edge straight from the video (`-vf crop=720:220:0:0`) and count dark pixels (luminance < 90) across the subject's columns in the top ~30 rows: zero = background there = CLEAR, a wide span = genuinely cut. Pixel counts decide geometry; vision reads decide presence, wording and identity.
- **A price-shaped detail IS a price.** A yellow/green shop sticker on the case's shrink wrap — or any price tag the model paints onto the product — counts as a price defect even when its figures are illegible. Report it as a sticker with its frame time; never fold it into "no price reached the screen", and never quote a figure you could not read. Both directions matter: an unread price must not be claimed present, and a present price-shaped tag must not be reported away.
- **A zoom settles only the frame it zoomed.** Two frames of the SAME date caption can return different years (2026 vs 2020) while both read the same Thai day/month line — the year is the model's preferred silent swap. When zooms disagree on a digit, that date's year is UNREADABLE: give Fix both candidates plus the brief's date, and do NOT pick the candidate that matches the brief — a hallucinated digit matching the brief is indistinguishable from the real one.
- **Two reads of ONE zoomed crop disagreeing about a printed detail is a cue to change the FRAME, not the wording.** Re-asking the same crop buys a third guess — instead sample a different frame where the product is held larger and ask the identical pointed question ("is there a red band across the case's top edge, transcribe it"). Agreement between two such frames is the verdict; continued disagreement means the detail is illegible, so quote every candidate with its frame time and let Fix judge from the clip. Never clear a frame on the single read that happened to agree with the brief.
- **A printed GAME TITLE that full-frame reads report as a different instalment is an OCR guess, not a wrong-game defect — and the numeral stays unverifiable even zoomed, so settle instalment identity on the ARTWORK, not the lettering.** Stylised cover lettering makes full-frame reads substitute the franchise's better-known entry (plus an invented subtitle) on the very frames where a zoom of the title region reads the product's own title, and a zoomed stylised numeral flips V/VI between calls while the panel artwork never moves. The discriminator is the panel content — the specific characters/animals/vehicles and the border colour — read off ONE labelled sheet carrying the frame crop, your reference crop, and the official cover art downloaded from the publisher or a wiki as controls: ask which control each crop matches BY ITS SUBJECTS, never "is it V or VI". Settle YOUR REFERENCE against the official art FIRST — reads of the reference flip the same way, and a clip judged against a misread reference is wrong in either direction — and treat title/numeral disagreement across reads as illegibility: quote every candidate with its frame time, never present the more famous title as what is on screen. **When a regen whose prompt explicitly named the cover spec reproduces the identical wrong artwork, the reference chip is being ignored, not misread:** report that to Fix with the credits spent and wait for his call — do not queue another regen on the same lever.
- **A printed STICKER whose text returns a NEW Thai string on every read is ILLEGIBLE — including shop-name-shaped strings.** Language-support notices and distributor seals read back as a different (sometimes store-shaped) name per read; the no-shop-name rule is violated only when two reads agree on a name. Report the candidates with frame times for Fix to judge from the clip: never fold an unread sticker into "no shop name reached the screen", and never assert the violation off one read either.
- **An on-screen SPEC the brief never stated is a factual defect of the same weight as a wrong date.** The model invents ratings and numbers that look native (`IPX6 Water` from a brief that only said กันน้ำ, a capacity, a size, a count), and the invented figure reaches the buyer as a claim. Compare every caption's claimed spec against the brief word by word, not just its digits: report it with its frame time as "ไม่ได้ระบุใน brief", and in the fix swap it for the brief's own wording (`Water Proof`) rather than deleting the beat. The other direction binds too — do not strip a spec that IS in the brief just because it looks like a number.
- **The PRODUCT itself can drift between shots while presenter, set and captions stay matched** — a transparent shell turns opaque, a printed LED strip, port or window disappears at one beat, because every shot is re-drawn from the reference rather than re-used. So ask each sampled frame the same property question the brief makes load-bearing (shell type, visible display/LED, ports, colourway, cover art) and, when the answer changes across frames, report the exact frame time and which property changed. Prevention belongs in the prompt, not only the check: a `PRODUCT LOCK` line naming the properties that must be visible in EVERY shot, repeated verbatim in each scene, is what keeps a key selling point from vanishing mid-clip.
- **The reference-vs-render identity comparison on a small source is a verdict for the shot it examines, not only a gate before the next submit.** A reference under ~1000 px gets redrawn as a plausible lookalike, so a part can come back wrong in EVERY frame while the render stays internally consistent — every between-frame question then passes on the same wrong part. When that side-by-side answers DRIFT on a structural part (display block, port count, strap, proportions), it goes to Fix in that shot's review message as a flag naming the part; only a clean comparison clears the next submit. Prevention is the product-structure block in the prompt (exact part counts plus the part's own look), which is what pins what the model otherwise redraws.
- **A product drawn at the wrong real-world scale fails the clip even when every shape question passes.** A kids' drawer unit rendered wardrobe-tall answers the drawer-count questions correctly and still fails Fix's review (size เลือกเกินจริงไปมาก). Prevention is prompt-side: real dimensions PLUS a body anchor (`its roof reaches only her HIP when she stands beside it`) — the no-size rule bans cm from VO/on-screen, not from the prompt. Verify per frame by asking where the product's top lands on the presenter (knees / hip / chest) against the reference photo's proportions, and report the frame time when it moves.
- **A full-frame read that reports a shop watermark or platform logo needs a ZOOM of that exact corner before it enters the delivery in EITHER direction.** A caption-height decorative graphic (a sparkle, star or sticker beside the caption) reads as a Shopee-style shopping-bag logo at full-frame scale, while a small real corner badge can vanish inside a prose description — so crop that corner (`ffmpeg -vf crop=W:H:X:Y,scale=2*`) and re-read it: only what survives the zoom is reportable, exactly as for price and date claims. A watermark verdict off one full-frame read is never a verdict — not to assert the violation, and not to clear the frame either.
- **Report which prompt-requested captions actually rendered.** The agent rewrite picks its own caption set and can silently drop a caption you asked for (a date caption like `Restock 24 Nov` never appearing while the other beats caption fine): absence is not a content defect, but the delivery names it — Fix needs to know the on-screen info carries less than the prompt asked, and a dropped date is exactly the claim a buyer looks for.
- **Garbled engraving on the product is not an on-screen-text defect when the real product carries engraving there.** Vision flags a lid's embossed brand mark (`KATHY AMREZ` re-drawn as `KATHYAMREZ`/`AVYA`) as "pseudo-text" because it applies the ON-SCREEN TEXT: NONE clause to the object itself. Check the source photo: if the real product has a mark in that spot, the re-drawn mark is product fidelity. At most, name it as "engraving reads soft" in the review. Only captions, overlays and invented labels count as text defects.
- **Presenter identity is a frame-check question on every P/R shot. Ask it about shot 1 BEFORE the review send (Fix caught it 2026-11-09: น้องเนย was attached, but the presenter rendered as น้องมิ้นท์ from shot 1 onward).** The chip alt only reads `Character ingredient image`, so it cannot prove which character is attached. **Settle WHICH file each chip is with pixels first — `flow-driver/scripts/chipcheck.py`, no vision call:** `uv run --with pillow --with numpy python -I /opt/data/skills/flow-driver/scripts/chipcheck.py --shot <capture> --rects '<the chips\' getBoundingClientRect() CSS px>' --expect <the refs + /opt/data/clips/characters/<name>.jpg> --pool-dir /opt/data/clips/characters` → exit 0 = every chip is the file you intended, exit 1 = fix the attach (no read), exit 3 = ONE vision read of only the chip it could not separate. The zoom it replaced cost one vision call per attach, which is what spends a job's budget before the frame sheet. Then put the chip image and the rendered frame side by side in ONE vision call and ask whether they show the same person (hair, face, outfit) — chipcheck proves what was ATTACHED, that pairing proves Flow HONOURED it; neither answers the other. **Compare on a frame where her FACE is unobstructed:** when she holds the product in front of her chin the same pair answers DIFFERENT (it guesses at age and outfit through the occlusion), while the face-clear frame answers SAME on every feature — so sample for a clear-face moment first, and let the clear-face read overrule the occluded one (the zoom rule above, applied to identity). **Getting the chip when nothing local has it:** over CDP on the open Flow project, click the `Characters` button, then walk each name `SPAN` up to ~5 ancestors for its `img.src` — each tile URL carries its OWN `Signature`, so copying one tile's query string onto another returns a 337-byte error body; download every full URL, lay the N cards out as a numbered PIL grid beside the rendered face, and ask which NUMBER matches. **A grid pick alone is not a verdict:** confirm with a second two-panel VIDEO-vs-chosen-card read; when the two disagree, identity is UNSETTLED — report it as unsettled with both candidates, never as a settled mismatch. A CHARACTER chip's `img.src` serves an HTML page instead of an image to a cookie-less fetch, so when the fetched bytes are HTML, crop the chip rect out of a prompt-box screenshot (PIL, 5–6x LANCZOS) and use that zoom. A mismatch is a defect: report it in the review message with the re-gen cost. In manual mode, name the character Fix must attach, and run the same comparison on his render.
- **When the chip and the render disagree, settle WHICH is wrong before reporting it — the two causes need different fixes.** Lay every roster portrait out as one numbered sheet (TILE 1..N) — numbered ONLY: printing the Thai names under
the tiles invites the reader to invent a Latin name in their place, and an invented name reads like a verdict;
keep the name↔number map in your code — beside one face-clear rendered frame and ask `which tile matches the
VIDEO tile, or NONE`: a match means the portrait file on disk is a wrong crop (repair the portrait before any
regen), while NONE means Flow rendered a face outside the roster — **but only once the chosen character's OWN
portrait file was among the tiles**: `/opt/data/clips/characters/` can lag `character_roster`, and without that
file the grid never tested the one name that matters, so confirm the file first; if it is missing, fetch that
character's chip over CDP and compare against it, or report identity as unresolved. When the grid really is
complete, NONE = the chip was never attached or not honoured, so the fix is the attach step, and another generation on the same setup returns the same stranger. Report the outcome with the mismatch; a regen spent on the wrong cause costs credits and changes nothing.
- **Ask each shot whether its planned ACTION happened, prop state included.** A presenter result shot can pass on look and voiceover while the compact stays closed and the eye and lip touches never happen, so the voiceover (`แก้ม ตา และปาก`) claims what the frames do not show. Put the planned action in the vision question verbatim, including open/closed state and each touch point. A miss is a defect for the review message, offered as a choice between keeping it (0 credits) and re-generating it (with the cost).
- **Thai captions read at thumbnail scale are not evidence of their wording.** Contact sheets and downscaled frames make the model misread Thai subtitles (a line that says `ดูหนัง` returns as `ดูหนิ่ง`). Use a contact sheet only to locate candidate time windows. Before a caption is reported as wrong, or passed, crop it at full resolution (`ffmpeg -ss T -i clip -frames:v 1`) and read that crop alone.
- **A printed spec on the PRODUCT at full-frame scale is equally not evidence — the label ZOOM overrules it.** A full-frame read of a softener jug's label returned `2000 ml` (drift flag vs the reference's `3000 ml`) while a LANCZOS zoom of the label region on the SAME frame returned `3000 ml.` and the variant name correctly. Volume, model and variant text printed on the product are read from a zoom of the label (PIL mask of the label hue, or crop the label rect, 3–4× upscale); a full-frame reading of small printed digits is a guess and never enters a verdict in either direction — it must not raise a drift flag, and it must not clear one.
- **Overlay text whose intended string is on disk (`gfx.json` headline, `subs.ass`, pills) is checked AGAINST that string, never transcribed blind — one line per crop.** Give the reader the candidate from the plan and ask MATCH/MISMATCH glyph-by-glyph on a `crop` of that single line's y band (y and size come from the gfx entry) at ~3×: free transcription of stylized Thai overlay invents a new syllable set on every call, and a full-band crop reads worse than a line crop. When candidate and reader disagree, the frame-change rule above applies — confirm on a second frame's crop before reporting a mismatch; never ship a mismatch verdict resting on one read. **For EN pills (Latin, and the ≤2-word / price-like gate turns on the exact printed string) skip vision: crop the pill band (`y 190–260` of the 1280 frame) at each pill's time from `work/timeline_v5.json` and RapidOCR it** — OCR returns the printed words verbatim, so `THIS SCENE` / `Link below!` and any digit or `%` come out as fact rather than a read; reserve vision for the Thai overlay lines and for presence/position questions.
- **A caption that lives only in the hook window (first ~2 s) survives mid/late sampling.** Frames at 4.5/8.0/9.3 s read clean while a Thai caption sat on screen from 0.3–2.0 s, so one of your samples must fall inside 0–2 s — and a text-ABSENCE verdict needs a band sweep, not full frames: crop the top and bottom ~260 px at 10–12 timestamps (`ffmpeg -ss T -i clip -frames:v 1 -vf crop=720:260:0:0`, and `crop=720:260:0:1020` for the bottom), tile each set `4x3`, and read every cell for presence. Two reads returning different strings for the same band mean the text is present but illegible (rule above): report the window, never the wording.
- **Count physical parts (tiers / slots / drawers) from a FULL frame, never from a crop.** A crop placed by eye clips the object, and wire racks are the worst case: thin rods drop out at the crop edge, so the SAME frame read through different crops returns 3, then 4, then 5 slots — each read internally consistent and all of them wrong. Take the count from one full-resolution frame (`count every slot top to bottom, including empty ones`), and use a crop only to confirm a specific ACTION (board seated, lid slotted into which slot). When a full frame and a crop disagree on the count, the crop is at fault: re-derive from the full frame and stop re-reading the crop — a second crop read buys another guess, not a verdict. Two FULL frames disagreeing with each other are likewise no arbiter: the reference photo of the real product is the only image that cannot be a render defect, so settle the count there — unless the reference shows SEVERAL units (a listing shot with two sizes side by side), which arbitrates nothing: settle that case on a crop that frames the WHOLE rendered unit edge to edge, and a crop that answers `CROP MISSES` is not a read — re-cut it wider (a full-width band across the object's rows) and ask once, never re-ask the same crop. Never count a part the clip is MOVING — spinning blades behind a wire grille blur into a plausible extra blade — take it from the last frame before the motion starts and confirm against the reference. Report occupancy, not just the count (`4 of 5 slots used`), so Fix sees what the clip actually shows. When the FIRST review sheet flags a count, confirm on a SECOND sheet spread across the clip's timeline before the verdict and name which frames carry the extras — extras appear and vanish between beats, so a count is a per-frame fact, never a clip-wide one.
- **A wordmark or logo that is mid-animation at the sampled frame is not a truncated product name.** Logo reveals and wipes are partial by design. Re-sample about 1 s later before flagging it, and report it as a flag only when the settled frame is also wrong.
- **Consistent ABSENCE of a reference token across every independent read is a flag, not a pass.** The illegibility rules say quote candidates and let Fix judge — but when frames and zooms keep returning a slot-by-slot near-miss (`OMEGA … DAILY` with a different third word each read) and NEVER the reference's own word (`TRIPLE`) that the previously approved shot showed clearly, the pattern itself goes into `--frame flag:` with its frame times. Small print at 720p (~200 px label) makes each single read a guess; only the pattern — same slots agreeing, reference token never appearing on any frame — is reportable, never one read alone.
- **A hand-held product parked at chest height is a framing defect, asked on every P/R shot.** The subtitle band (y 700–835 of 1280, 55–65 % down the frame) is burned over that height in post, so the label ends up under the Thai text (venita-sunscreen 2026-11-16: `SPF 50 PA+++` covered for 7 of 10 s; no script gate sees it). Add to the 3-frame question: `is the product's label between 55 % and 65 % of the frame height?` Yes on 2 of 3 frames = tell Fix in the review message (`ฉลากจะอยู่ใต้ซับ`) with 🔁 as his option; it is his call, not an automatic re-gen. Prevention is the holding-height line in `templates/clip-prompt.md`.
- The rest of the verdict rules (caption garble = defect, claim-word flips, stray props, cover-art fidelity, multi-scene seam continuity) stay in SKILL.md step 10.

## Sending the review
- **One shot's `ready` at a time.** Two review keyboards in flight make the answer ambiguous (which ✅/🔁 is he tapping?), so shot K's review goes out only after shot K−1 is decided; run shot K+1's frame checks while he answers, so the wait costs nothing.
- **A shot flag's `clarify` that times out is the zero-spend KEEP, and it must be recorded:** run
  `clipjob.py decide <job> K approve` before moving on, or the shot stays `in_review` and `deliver` refuses
  with "unapproved shot" long after the run stopped being able to ask. Same for report flags at delivery:
  `--ack "<flag>=zero-spend default (clarify timed out)"` — the ack must say it was the timeout default, never
  that Fix chose it.

## Locating a crop
A crop guessed from memory lands on the wrong region (a torso where the fan head was) and burns a vision call.
- **Probe the pixels FIRST whenever the target has a detectable signature** — a saturated product hue, a near-white
  cord/strap, dark printed marks on a light body: mask the predicate over a step-2 PIL scan, take the mask's bbox,
  pad outward, cut — zero vision round-trips. Require a per-row AND per-column hit-count floor (≥6) so scattered
  matches do not set the bbox: a loose hue range also catches a burned-in pill/subtitle fill and antialiased text,
  which returns a bbox spanning the full frame width; tighten the predicate (raise the red floor, lower the green/
  blue ceilings) until the mask sits on the product alone. A grid-sheet vision bbox came back in the wrong region
  even with coordinate labels drawn on the sheet, so treat vision's box as a guess to be corrected by the probe,
  never as the cut line.
- With no usable signature: `ffmpeg -i f.jpg -vf "drawgrid=w=80:h=80:t=1:c=red@0.6" fg.jpg`, ask vision for the
  pixel bbox `[x1,y1,x2,y2]` of the target using the grid lines as the ruler, then `crop=W:H:X:Y,scale=4*` from those
  numbers. Bbox estimates run generous — pad rather than tighten, and re-ask with a narrower question if the crop
  misses; the goal is landing on the right object, not a tight frame.
- Either way, close with ONE vision call on the CUT crop asking what it shows — the crop itself is the verdict that
  the region was right; a wrong box that nobody verified costs a full rebuild round later.

## When vision reads fail mid-run
- Send vision a small JPEG (`scripts/vprep.py <img>` → `<img>.v.jpg`), one `vision_analyze` per tool batch. Before a message that ends the turn while the job is open: `clipjob.py wait <job> "<what is needed>"`.
- A provider-level `vision_analyze` failure (HTTP 400 naming an unsupported model, or repeated timeouts) is a transient work-stop, not a dead run — and never a serial-retry loop: probe once, then move on.
  - "Probe once" = re-send the SAME sheet downscaled to ≤1800 px wide with a one-line question (a full-resolution 2880 px sheet times out on its size, and the resized copy answers in seconds), then — only if that fails — ONE retry with the question cut to its core: a timeout on a large multi-tile sheet is often the long question rather than the provider, and the same image can return normally under a 1-line question. If that trimmed probe also fails, the provider is down — move on, no third attempt.
- Everything except the pixel gate runs without vision: render poll, download, `verify-clip.py`, log/state writes, and the next clip's submit. Do that work while the provider recovers, then slot the pending frame reads back in.
- If it never recovers in this run, state exactly which frame times went unread in the delivery — an unrun frame check is reported as NOT RUN, never as clean and never as an implied pass.

## Past the vision budget
Decide from substitutes and `note` which you used: RapidOCR boxes for text bounding boxes (card/logo layout, crop
margins), row/column brightness probes for edges and for "is there content up here", a 10 ms RMS envelope for
audio cut timing, pixel-diff means for "did that overlay render". The post frame sheet and the cover read are not
optional: when the count says the budget is spent, check `clipjob.py status <job>` → `vision` first (a queued
job's count was inflated by the job ahead until 2026-11-27).
